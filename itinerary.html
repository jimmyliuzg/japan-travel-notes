The route

Tokyo → Fukuoka → Arita & Okawachiyama → Harazuru Onsen → Fukuoka

18 NovWednesday

Travel day

Set off for Japan

Keep this day for the long journey. Rest when possible and leave plans open until arrival.

Location

In transit

Food

Keep it easy; no meal plans set.

19 NovThursday

Tokyo · arrival

Arrive, settle in, and take a short walk

Get to the hotel, stretch your legs in the neighborhood, and call it an early night.

Locations

Tokyo; neighborhood near the hotel.

Food

Casual dinner nearby; keep the choice flexible.

20 NovFriday

Tokyo · run, vintage, wine

A morning run, browsing, and one special dinner

  • Run near the Imperial Palace.
  • Browse vintage luxury and design in Aoyama, Daikanyama, or Ginza.
  • Find a Japanese wine bar; choose one counter-seat dinner.
Locations

Imperial Palace area; Aoyama, Daikanyama, or Ginza (choose one or two).

Food

Japanese wine; a reserved counter dinner (cuisine not yet specified).

21 NovSaturday

Tokyo · culture and Ginza

One museum or design anchor

  • Choose Intermediatheque, or the Edo-Tokyo Open Air Architectural Museum if the weather is good; another mood-led museum is also fine.
  • Take an easy Ginza afternoon for a food hall, watches, stationery, or a long lunch.
Locations

Intermediatheque or Edo-Tokyo Open Air Architectural Museum; Ginza.

Food

Ginza food hall or a long lunch; keep dinner open.

22 NovSunday

Tokyo · flex day

Keep the day unstructured

  • Wander Kiyosumi-Shirakawa for coffee and design, join a guided food-and-wine walk, return to shopping, or choose a second wine bar.
  • Skip a long day trip; let the day stay flexible.
Locations

Kiyosumi-Shirakawa is one option; otherwise follow the day’s mood.

Food

Coffee; optional guided food-and-wine walk or wine bar.

23 NovMonday

Tokyo → Fukuoka

Travel south; keep the afternoon light

  • Leave early for the airport transfer; this is Japan’s Labor Thanksgiving Day, so allow extra time.
  • After arrival and check-in, visit Ohori Park or the Fukuoka Art Museum if energy allows.
Locations

Tokyo; Fukuoka; Ohori Park or Fukuoka Art Museum.

Food

Choose a seafood, modern Japanese, or yakitori dinner (one option, not all).

24 NovTuesday

Fukuoka · food and spa

Park time, a food walk, and one bath

  • Run at Ohori Park.
  • Take a small-group food walk focused on neighborhood context and local specialties.
  • Choose either the hotel spa or Namiha no Yu; not both.
Locations

Ohori Park; Namiha no Yu or the hotel spa.

Food

Tonkotsu ramen for lunch; seafood, gyoza, and yakitori on the walk; motsunabe for dinner.

25 NovWednesday

Arita & Okawachiyama → Harazuru

Ceramics, then a quiet onsen evening

  • Visit an Arita showroom or kiln with a private guide/driver, including a local lunch.
  • Continue to Okawachiyama village for studios and galleries.
  • Head to Harazuru Onsen by mid-afternoon; leave the evening clear for a private-bath slot and rest.
Locations

Arita; Okawachiyama village; Harazuru Onsen.

Food

Local lunch; kaiseki dinner at the ryokan.

26 NovThursday

Harazuru Onsen → Fukuoka

Take a slow morning and return to the city

  • Have a slow breakfast and one final bath.
  • Transfer to Hakata or another convenient Fukuoka base for the final night.
  • Pack, keep the evening low-key, and get an early night.
Locations

Harazuru Onsen; Hakata or another convenient Fukuoka area.

Food

Breakfast at the inn; a simple final dinner in the city.

27 NovFriday

Return journey

Leave a generous connection buffer

  • Travel from Fukuoka to the Tokyo-area connection point.
  • Collect checked luggage and allow time to check in separately for the onward journey.
  • Keep the day focused on a smooth trip home; no extra sightseeing planned.
Locations

Fukuoka; Tokyo-area connection point. Origin city and airport details are not shown.

Food

Airport or travel-day meals as convenient; no specific stop planned.

A few trip rules

  • One anchor per day; a second activity is optional.
  • Keep transfer days light and build in buffers.
  • Plan two or three destination meals plus the ryokan dinner; keep the rest spontaneous.
  • Yatai is a snack-and-drink experience, not the main dinner plan.
  • Use taxis or private transfers where they meaningfully reduce friction; pack layers and a rain shell for the onsen countryside.
